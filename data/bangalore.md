## Coverage and format norms
- The Karnataka Rent Act, 1999 historically applied to residential buildings with standard rent above roughly **₹3,500/month** within municipal-corporation-covered areas (an old figure, likely outdated — **VERIFY**).
- As in Pune, most actual Bangalore rentals are **informal, contract-governed 11-month agreements**, not full statutory tenancies, specifically to avoid the registration requirement that kicks in at 12 months/over 11 months.

## Clause-by-clause benchmark

### 1. Rent and escalation
- **Standard:** rent amount fixed by agreement; the Act defines a concept of "standard rent" to prevent excessive charges for covered premises, and permissible increases follow the Act's rules for those premises.
- **Possible recent change (unconfirmed):** a requirement of 90 days' written notice before any rent increase — flag as "possible, pending verification" rather than citing as settled law.

### 2. Security deposit and refund
- **Baseline figure found across multiple sources (more consistently reported):** residential deposits historically ran up to **10 months' rent**, commercial up to **12 months' rent**, as an unregulated market practice rather than a statutory cap.
- **Possible recent change (unconfirmed):** a 2-month cap for residential deposits, aligned with the Model Tenancy Act.
- **Recommendation for the tool:** present both figures with their sourcing caveat, and mark any deposit clause between 2 and 10 months as "verify against current law" rather than auto-labelling it Standard or Unfair, since which cap is legally correct is precisely the open question here.

### 3. Lock-in period
- Contractual; same fairness benchmark (asymmetric lock-in binding only the tenant) applies.

### 4. Notice period and termination
- **Standard, per the Act (subject to confirming current section numbering):** landlord must give the tenant at least **3 months' notice** before eviction, unless the tenant has committed a serious breach of the tenancy agreement.
- **Common contractual practice for 11-month informal agreements:** notice period of 1–3 months as stated in the agreement, which may differ from the statutory 3-month figure since these informal agreements often sit outside the Act's direct coverage — **VERIFY which regime actually governs a given short-form Bangalore lease.**

### 5. Eviction terms
- **Standard, per the Act:** tenant can challenge an eviction order in court (a specific right-to-challenge provision was referenced in secondary sources); courts are directed to promote negotiated settlement of landlord-tenant disputes before proceeding to a contested order.
- **Flag as Unfair:** any clause allowing the landlord to bypass this process (lock-changing, forcible entry, utility cut-off as pressure).
- Source: Karnataka Rent Act, 1999 — provisions on notice period, right to challenge eviction, and court-encouraged settlement were referenced with specific section numbers in a secondary source; these numbers are **not independently re-verified against the bare act** in this pass and should not be cited to end users until confirmed.

### 6. Maintenance and repairs
- No Karnataka-specific statutory repair provision was distinctly surfaced in this pass; apply the general Transfer of Property Act §108 default (landlord = structural, tenant = day-to-day) pending confirmation of a Karnataka-specific rule.

### 7. Utilities and charges
- No statute-specific provision surfaced; apply the cross-jurisdiction norm against utility disconnection as a pressure tactic during a dispute — this is echoed as an explicitly "illegal clause" example in at least one 2026 secondary source discussing the possible amendment (**VERIFY**).

### 8. Subletting
- Treat as a standard prohibition unless the agreement expressly permits it; no distinct Karnataka statutory citation surfaced in this pass.

### 9. Landlord entry and privacy
- **Possible recent change (unconfirmed):** 24 hours' notice requirement for landlord entry, per the possible 2026 amendment discussed above. Until confirmed, apply the general cross-jurisdiction fairness benchmark (notice required except emergencies) rather than citing a specific hour figure.

### 10. Registration and stamp duty
- **Standard, well-corroborated across many sources:** agreements up to 11 months do not require registration and typically attract a flat e-stamp duty (a figure around ₹200 was widely cited for short-form agreements, but **VERIFY current amount**, since stamp duty schedules are periodically revised). Agreements of 12 months or more require registration at the Sub-Registrar's Office (or, per recent sources, the Kaveri portal), with stamp duty calculated as a small percentage of total rent over the term.
- Police verification of the tenant is commonly described as a requirement in Bengaluru specifically — **VERIFY this is a legal requirement and not just common landlord practice.**

### 11. Penalties and fines

### 12. Dispute resolution
- **Standard:** Rent Authority/Controller mechanism under the Act for covered tenancies; ordinary civil court or negotiated settlement for informal short-form agreements outside the Act's direct coverage.
