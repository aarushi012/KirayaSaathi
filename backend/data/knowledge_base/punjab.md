# Punjab Tenancy & Rental Legal Framework

## Statutory Overview
- **Primary Legislation**: Punjab Rent Act, 1995 & East Punjab Urban Rent Restriction Act, 1949 (as amended).
- **Enforcement Authority**: Rent Authorities / Appellate Rent Tribunals, Department of Housing & Urban Development, Government of Punjab.
- **Police Jurisdiction**: Mandatory tenant registration through Punjab Police Saanjh Kendra Portal.

---

## 1. Security Deposit Regulations & Benchmarks
- **Standard Ceiling**: 1 to 2 months' rent for urban residential tenancies (Ludhiana, Amritsar, Jalandhar, Mohali/SAS Nagar, Patiala).
- **Maximum Legal Benchmark**: Demands exceeding 2 months' rent are contrary to Punjab Model Tenancy recommendations.
- **Statutory Refund Timeline**: Refund must be finalized within 30 days of receiving vacant possession.
- **Permissible Deductions**: Documented electricity/water dues, municipal charges, and verifiable tenant-caused damage.
- **Unlawful Retention**: Retaining deposit as arbitrary liquidated damages when replacement tenants are promptly found is prohibited.

---

## 2. Mandatory Notice Periods & Eviction Safeguards
- **Notice to Vacate**: Minimum 30 days (1 month) written notice for monthly tenancies; 2 months recommended for fixed annual leases.
- **Grounds for Eviction**: Section 20 of Punjab Rent Act strictly defines valid eviction grounds:
  - Material non-payment of rent despite 15 days formal demand notice.
  - Sub-letting premises without written consent of landlord.
  - Substantial damage or structural impairment to property.
- **Illegal Dispossession**: Section 38 penalizes landlords who illegally cut off essential amenities (water, electricity, access pathways).

---

## 3. Lock-in Period Standards
- **Standard Benchmark**: 1 to 3 months for residential agreements.
- **Excessive Lock-in**: Clauses restricting tenant exit for 12 months with full deposit forfeiture are deemed punitive.
- **Exit Relief**: Tenants facing job transfer, educational relocation, or health emergencies must be granted early exit with 30-day notice.

---

## 4. Maintenance & Repair Allocation
- **Landlord Obligations**:
  - Whitewashing / painting structural elements every 2–3 years.
  - Structural foundation repairs, roof treatment, external sewage mains.
  - Water tank replacements and motor pumps pre-installed.
- **Tenant Obligations**:
  - Interior cleanliness, operational servicing of taps, switches, and fuses.
  - Tenant liability cap for routine repairs: ₹500 to ₹1,000 per occurrence.

---

## 5. Landlord Inspection & Entry Rights
- **Advance Notice**: Minimum 24 hours prior written or verbal notice.
- **Inspection Hours**: Permitted only between sunrise and sunset (8:00 AM to 7:00 PM).
- **Privacy Rights**: Landlords cannot conduct arbitrary or weekly intrusive inspections.

---

## 6. Rent Revision & Escalation
- **Standard Benchmark**: 5% to 8% annual escalation for residential tenancies across Punjab urban centers.
- **Unilateral Revisions**: Increases cannot exceed agreed contractual rates without formal written mutual addendum.

---

## 7. Sub-letting & Commercial Conversion
- **Sub-letting Rule**: Prohibited unless express written consent is granted by the property owner.
- **Residential Usage**: Using premises for commercial retail without zoning permits can trigger eviction under urban municipal bylaws.

---

## 8. Common Predatory Clauses & Negotiation Scripts

### Predatory Clause: Unlimited Tenant Repair Liability
> *"Tenant is responsible for all structural, plumbing, motor and electrical repairs during tenancy."*
- **Tenant Counter**: *"Could we clarify that the landlord handles structural and major equipment repairs while tenant covers minor daily consumables up to ₹500?"*

### Predatory Clause: Summary Eviction Without Notice
> *"Landlord may demand immediate vacancy within 24 hours upon any rule breach."*
- **Tenant Counter**: *"Could we align this clause with standard Punjab tenancy guidelines requiring at least 15 days written notice to cure any minor concern?"*
